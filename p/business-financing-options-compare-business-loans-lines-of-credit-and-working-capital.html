Business Financing Options: Compare Business Loans, Lines of Credit and Working Capital

Emily Carter
Investment Consultant · October 9, 2026

Business financing can help companies cover operating expenses, purchase equipment, manage cash flow and fund expansion. From traditional business loans to flexible lines of credit, the available options differ in interest rates, eligibility requirements, repayment terms and overall costs. Understanding these differences can help business owners compare financing solutions, evaluate potential lenders and identify funding that aligns with their business needs.

Introduction

Access to business financing can determine whether a company can purchase inventory, cover operating expenses, hire employees, or pursue its next growth opportunity. However, choosing the wrong financing product can create repayment obligations that put pressure on cash flow.

Business owners can choose from several funding options, including traditional business loans, unsecured business loans, business lines of credit, equipment financing, invoice financing and merchant cash advances. Each option has different eligibility requirements, borrowing costs, repayment structures and approval processes.

The right choice depends on how much capital a business needs, how quickly it needs funding, its financial history and its ability to repay the borrowed amount. Comparing multiple financing options before applying can help business owners identify suitable terms and avoid unnecessary expenses.

Compare Business Financing Options

Different financing products serve different business needs. Before submitting an application, compare the main features of each option.

  • Term business loan: Common purpose: expansion, investment and major expenses. Important consideration: scheduled principal and interest payments.
  • Unsecured business loan: Common purpose: working capital and general business expenses. Important consideration: pricing may reflect higher lender risk.
  • Business line of credit: Common purpose: short-term cash flow gaps. Important consideration: interest and fees depend on usage and terms.
  • Equipment financing: Common purpose: machinery, vehicles and technology. Important consideration: equipment may serve as collateral.
  • Invoice financing: Common purpose: accessing funds tied up in unpaid invoices. Important consideration: advance amounts and fees vary.
  • Merchant cash advance: Common purpose: short-term funding repaid from sales. Important consideration: total repayment costs can be substantial.

Availability, product names and regulatory protections vary by country and provider. Businesses should compare offers available in their own market rather than assuming that every product operates under identical rules.

1. Business Term Loans

A business term loan provides a specified amount of money that the borrower repays according to an agreed schedule. Depending on the lender and agreement, repayment may include fixed or variable interest, fees and other financing costs.

Term loans can suit businesses planning substantial expenditures, such as opening a new location, purchasing inventory in bulk or investing in additional production capacity.

What determines business loan eligibility?

Lenders commonly evaluate several factors:

  • Time in business: Established companies may have more financial records available for assessment.
  • Revenue and cash flow: Lenders examine whether income can support the proposed repayments.
  • Credit history: Business and owner credit records may influence approval and pricing.
  • Existing debt: Current financial obligations affect repayment capacity.
  • Collateral: Some financing arrangements require assets to secure the loan.

Requirements vary by lender. Some providers consider newer businesses, while others prefer companies with an established trading history.

How much does a business loan cost?

The advertised interest rate is only one part of the borrowing cost. Origination fees, administration charges, required insurance and other contractual expenses can increase the total amount payable.

Before accepting an offer, compare the annual percentage rate (APR), where available, the repayment schedule, total repayment amount and any additional charges. If a provider quotes a factor rate rather than an interest rate, ask for a clear breakdown of the total repayment obligation.

2. Unsecured Business Loans

An unsecured business loan generally does not require specific business assets to be pledged as collateral. This can make it attractive to companies that lack substantial property or equipment to secure financing.

However, unsecured does not necessarily mean risk-free. A lender may require a personal guarantee, impose restrictive contract terms or charge a higher rate to compensate for the perceived risk.

Eligibility may depend on revenue, creditworthiness, operating history and existing liabilities. Some lenders also require a minimum monthly turnover or a business bank account.

Before applying, confirm whether the agreement includes a personal guarantee, default charges, early repayment fees or other conditions that could affect the owner's personal financial exposure.

3. Business Lines of Credit

A business line of credit allows an eligible company to access funds up to an approved limit, subject to the agreement. Instead of receiving the entire amount upfront, the business can draw funds when needed.

This flexibility can help businesses manage seasonal fluctuations, delayed customer payments or unexpected operating expenses.

For example, a wholesaler might use a credit line to purchase inventory before a busy sales period and repay the balance as customers pay their invoices.

The total cost depends on the amount drawn, interest rate, fees and repayment terms. Some facilities also require periodic reviews or renewal.

A line of credit is generally worth considering when a business needs flexible access to working capital rather than a single lump sum. However, repeatedly borrowing to cover persistent operating losses may create a longer-term debt problem.

4. Equipment Financing

Equipment financing is designed for purchasing business assets such as commercial vehicles, machinery, manufacturing equipment and technology.

The equipment may serve as collateral, depending on the financing arrangement. This can influence the lender's eligibility criteria, pricing and recovery rights if the borrower defaults.

When comparing equipment financing offers, examine the deposit, repayment period, interest and fees, maintenance responsibilities and ownership conditions. Also consider whether the equipment is likely to generate sufficient additional revenue or savings to justify its cost.

Businesses should compare financing with purchasing the asset outright or leasing it. The most appropriate structure depends on cash reserves, expected usage, asset lifespan and the full cost of each alternative.

5. Invoice Financing and Working Capital Funding

Businesses that sell on credit may experience cash shortages while waiting for customers to pay. Invoice financing can provide access to funds against eligible outstanding invoices, subject to the provider's terms.

Depending on the product, a provider may advance a portion of an invoice's value and collect repayment when the customer pays. Fees, advance percentages, recourse arrangements and customer-notification requirements differ.

Working capital financing is a broader category covering funding used for everyday operations, such as payroll, inventory and supplier payments.

Before selecting either option, estimate how much funding is needed and how long the cash-flow gap is expected to last. Short-term financing can be useful when repayments align with incoming revenue, but repeated financing charges can erode margins.

6. Merchant Cash Advances

A merchant cash advance provides funding in exchange for an agreed portion of future sales or another contractual repayment arrangement. Availability and legal treatment vary by jurisdiction.

This type of funding may appeal to businesses with regular card transactions that need relatively quick access to capital. However, it can be expensive compared with other financing options.

Review the total repayment amount, collection percentage, estimated repayment period, fees and consequences of slower-than-expected sales. Do not compare a factor rate directly with an annual interest rate without converting the offers into comparable cost measures.

A fast approval process does not necessarily indicate an affordable financing arrangement.

What Do Lenders Look for When Approving Business Financing?

Lender requirements differ, but applications commonly involve an assessment of the business's financial position and ability to repay.

Applicants may be asked to provide:

  • Business registration and ownership details.
  • Recent business bank statements.
  • Financial statements and tax records.
  • Revenue, profit and cash-flow information.
  • Details of existing loans and liabilities.
  • Identification and credit information.
  • Information about the intended use of funds.

Some online lenders use digital application processes, while banks and other institutions may require additional documentation.

Prepare accurate records before applying and check the lender's minimum requirements. Submitting applications to providers whose eligibility criteria do not match the business can waste time and potentially result in unnecessary credit inquiries.

How to Compare Business Loan Offers

The lowest advertised interest rate is not always the cheapest financing option. A meaningful comparison should consider the complete cost and the effect of repayments on business cash flow.

Use the following checklist when reviewing offers:

  1. 1Total repayment: How much will the business repay over the entire agreement?
  2. 2APR and fees: What is the effective annual borrowing cost, where an APR is available?
  3. 3Repayment frequency: Are payments due daily, weekly, monthly or on another schedule?
  4. 4Collateral and guarantees: Are business assets or personal guarantees required?
  5. 5Early repayment: Are there penalties or restrictions on paying the balance early?
  6. 6Funding timeline: When can funds realistically become available after approval?
  7. 7Default provisions: What happens if payments are late or the business experiences financial difficulties?

Request written terms and compare equivalent borrowing amounts and repayment periods. If offers use different pricing methods, ask each provider for the total amount payable and a clear schedule of all fees.

How Much Business Financing Can You Afford?

The amount a lender is willing to offer is not necessarily the amount a business should borrow.

Start by estimating the specific funding requirement. Then forecast cash inflows, operating expenses, existing debt payments and the proposed new repayment obligation.

For example, a business considering financing for additional inventory should estimate the expected sales margin, the time required to sell the inventory and the date payment is likely to be received. A delay in sales could make even a seemingly affordable loan difficult to repay.

Stress-test the forecast by considering lower sales, delayed customer payments and unexpected expenses. The business should understand how repayments would be covered if its projections are not achieved.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a new business qualify for a loan?

Some lenders offer financing to startups, while others require a minimum operating history, established revenue or collateral. New businesses may need to provide a detailed business plan, owner financial information or alternative evidence of repayment capacity.

Are unsecured business loans better than secured loans?

Neither option is universally better. Unsecured financing may avoid pledging specific assets, but it can have higher costs or require a personal guarantee. Secured financing may offer different pricing and borrowing limits, depending on the lender and collateral.

How quickly can a business receive funding?

Timelines range from relatively fast decisions for some online products to longer underwriting and documentation processes for other facilities. Approval does not always mean funds will be disbursed immediately.

Can a business get financing with bad credit?

Some lenders consider applicants with weaker credit profiles, but approval is not guaranteed. Financing may be more expensive or require additional security. Compare the total cost carefully before accepting an offer.

What documents are needed to apply?

Common requirements include business registration documents, bank statements, financial records, identification and details of existing liabilities. The exact list depends on the provider, product and jurisdiction.

Final Thoughts

Business financing can support growth, manage temporary cash-flow gaps and fund essential investments. However, the right product depends on the purpose of borrowing, the company's financial position and its capacity to meet repayment obligations.

Compare term loans, unsecured financing, credit lines, equipment loans and alternative working capital products using total repayment costs, eligibility requirements, fees and contractual conditions. Reviewing several suitable offers can help business owners make more informed financing decisions.
