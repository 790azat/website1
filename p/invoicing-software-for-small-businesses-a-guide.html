Invoicing Software for Small Businesses: A Guide

James Mitchell
Financial Specialist · April 10, 2025

For many small businesses, creating invoices, following up on unpaid bills, and keeping payment records can take significant time. As the number of customers and transactions grows, managing invoices manually can become increasingly difficult.

Invoicing software for small businesses provides digital tools for creating, sending, tracking, and managing invoices. Depending on the platform, it may also connect invoicing with online payments, accounting, expense tracking, time tracking, and financial reporting.

Choosing the right solution depends on the business's size, billing process, customers, payment methods, and accounting needs. Understanding the main features and considerations can help business owners select software that fits their existing workflow.

What Is Invoicing Software?

Invoicing software is a digital application that helps businesses prepare and manage invoices electronically. Instead of creating invoices manually with spreadsheets or documents, businesses can use templates to enter customer information, products or services, prices, taxes, payment terms, and other details.

Modern invoicing platforms can also track whether invoices have been sent, viewed, paid, or become overdue. Some solutions allow businesses to automate recurring invoices and payment reminders, reducing the amount of manual follow-up required.

Many invoicing products are also connected to broader accounting platforms. This can allow invoice and payment information to flow into financial records without requiring the business owner to enter every transaction manually.

Key Features of Invoicing Software

Invoice Creation and Templates

Most platforms provide customizable invoice templates. Businesses can typically add their logo, business information, customer details, line items, payment terms, taxes, and other relevant information.

Templates can help maintain a consistent format across invoices while reducing the time required to create each document.

Invoice Tracking

Invoice tracking allows businesses to monitor the status of outstanding invoices. Depending on the software, users may be able to see whether an invoice was sent, viewed, paid, or overdue.

This can make it easier to identify unpaid balances and prioritize follow-up.

Online Payments

Many invoicing platforms allow customers to pay directly from an electronic invoice. Supported payment methods vary by provider but can include credit cards, bank transfers, and other digital payment options.

For businesses that regularly collect payments online, integrated payment processing can reduce the number of steps between sending an invoice and receiving payment.

Automated Reminders

Payment reminders can be particularly useful when a business manages numerous customers. Software may allow reminders to be scheduled before or after an invoice's due date.

Automating routine reminders can reduce administrative work while helping businesses maintain more consistent payment follow-up.

Recurring Invoices

Recurring invoicing is useful for businesses that charge customers on a regular schedule. Examples include subscription businesses, consultants, agencies, maintenance providers, and other service companies with repeat billing arrangements.

Instead of creating each invoice manually, the software can generate invoices according to a predefined schedule.

Time and Expense Tracking

Some platforms connect invoices with billable hours and business expenses. This can be useful for consultants, freelancers, contractors, and professional service businesses that charge clients based on time or reimbursable expenses.

Tracked hours or expenses can sometimes be transferred directly to an invoice, reducing duplicate data entry.

Reporting and Accounting Integration

Invoicing software may include reports covering outstanding invoices, payments, revenue, and other financial information. When integrated with accounting software, invoice and payment data can also contribute to broader bookkeeping processes.

For example, QuickBooks connects invoicing with accounting and payment features, while Xero combines invoicing with bank reconciliation and financial reporting.

Benefits of Invoicing Software for Small Businesses

Saves Administrative Time

Creating invoices manually can involve repetitive data entry and follow-up. Templates, saved customer information, recurring invoices, and automated reminders can reduce these tasks.

Helps Track Outstanding Payments

Centralized invoice records make it easier to identify unpaid and overdue balances. This can give business owners a clearer view of amounts that still need to be collected.

Supports Faster Payment Processes

Online payment options can make it easier for customers to pay directly from an invoice. Some platforms also provide notifications when invoices are viewed or paid.

Reduces Manual Errors

Automated calculations, reusable customer information, and connected accounting records can reduce some types of data-entry mistakes. Businesses should still review invoices before sending them, particularly when taxes, discounts, or complex billing arrangements are involved.

Improves Financial Organization

Keeping invoices, payments, and related financial information in one system can make recordkeeping more organized. Integration with accounting software can further reduce the need to maintain separate records.

Invoicing Software vs. Accounting Software

Invoicing software and accounting software overlap, but they are not necessarily the same.

A dedicated invoicing solution may focus primarily on creating invoices, collecting payments, and tracking customer balances. Accounting software typically covers a broader range of financial activities, such as income and expense tracking, bank reconciliation, financial reporting, and tax-related records.

For a freelancer or very small business with straightforward billing requirements, an invoicing-focused tool may be sufficient. A growing company may prefer an accounting platform that includes invoicing as one part of a larger financial system.

Examples of Invoicing Software

Several established platforms offer invoicing features for small businesses.

QuickBooks provides customizable invoices, payment tracking, automated reminders, recurring invoices, and connections between payments and accounting records.

Xero offers online invoicing alongside accounting features, payment options, invoice-status tracking, recurring invoices, and automated payment reminders.

FreshBooks combines invoicing with features such as time tracking, expense tracking, online payments, recurring invoices, and financial reporting.

The appropriate choice depends on the business's requirements rather than the number of features available. A company should evaluate how well each platform fits its existing billing and accounting processes.

Key Considerations When Choosing Invoicing Software

Business Size and Billing Volume

A freelancer sending a few invoices each month may have different requirements from a company managing hundreds of invoices. Consider the expected number of customers, invoices, users, and transactions.

Payment Methods

Check which payment methods are supported and whether additional transaction fees apply. Businesses serving customers in different countries should also review currency and payment availability.

Accounting Integration

If the business already uses accounting software, compatibility can be important. Connecting invoicing and accounting systems may reduce duplicate data entry and simplify financial recordkeeping.

Automation

Consider whether the software supports recurring invoices, scheduled reminders, payment notifications, and other automated workflows that match the business's billing process.

Mobile Access

Mobile access can be useful for businesses that invoice customers away from the office. Many modern platforms provide mobile applications or browser-based access.

Security and Data Management

Invoices contain customer and business information, so businesses should review available security controls, user permissions, authentication options, and data-management practices.

Pricing

Compare subscription costs with payment-processing fees and charges for additional users or features. A low monthly price does not necessarily mean a lower overall cost if the business requires multiple paid add-ons.

Final Thoughts

Invoicing software can help small businesses create professional invoices, track payments, automate routine billing tasks, and maintain more organized financial records. Features such as online payments, recurring invoices, reminders, time tracking, and accounting integration can make the billing process more efficient.

The right solution depends on the business's billing volume, payment requirements, accounting workflow, budget, and plans for growth. Evaluating these factors before choosing a platform can help ensure that invoicing software supports the business without adding unnecessary complexity.
