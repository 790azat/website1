Businesses manage expenses across travel, meals, office supplies, software subscriptions, mileage, client activities, and other operating costs. As the number of employees and transactions grows, collecting receipts and reviewing expense reports through email and spreadsheets can become increasingly difficult.

Expense management software provides a centralized system for capturing, submitting, reviewing, approving, reimbursing, and reporting business expenses. Modern platforms can also connect with corporate cards, accounting systems, ERP platforms, and travel-management tools.

The right solution can reduce manual administrative work and give finance teams greater visibility into company spending. However, businesses should evaluate workflow requirements, policy controls, integrations, security, reporting, pricing, and scalability before selecting a platform.

## What Is Expense Management Software?

Expense management software is a digital system that helps businesses track and control employee and company expenses. It can manage the process from the initial receipt or transaction through approval, reimbursement, and accounting.

Typical expense-management workflows involve expense submission, receipt capture, policy checks, approval, reimbursement, and financial reporting. Microsoft describes expense management as "a process that can provide an auditable path from an employee's expense through approval and posting to the general ledger."

The software can be particularly useful for organizations with employees who travel, purchase supplies, use corporate cards, or regularly incur reimbursable expenses.

## Key Features of Expense Management Software

### Receipt Capture

Receipt capture allows employees to submit receipts digitally rather than retaining paper documents and manually entering every transaction.

Modern systems can use OCR or AI-based data extraction to identify information such as the merchant, amount, date, and expense category. Microsoft and SAP Concur both describe automated receipt capture and data extraction as features of their expense-management systems.

### Expense Reporting

Employees can create expense reports that group individual transactions together for review and reimbursement.

Reports can include receipts, expense categories, business purposes, project codes, mileage, attendees, and other information required by company policy.

### Approval Workflows

Expense software can route reports to managers or other designated approvers according to predefined rules.

For example, an expense above a certain threshold could require additional approval, while routine expenses could follow a simpler workflow. Configurable approval processes are a common feature of modern expense-management platforms.

### Expense Policy Controls

Businesses can establish rules covering spending categories, limits, required receipts, approved merchants, travel expenses, and other requirements.

Software can automatically flag or prevent certain out-of-policy expenses before they are approved. This can help finance teams apply spending policies more consistently.

### Corporate Card Integration

Connecting corporate cards with expense software can automatically import transactions into the expense-management system.

Employees can then match receipts to card transactions rather than entering every transaction manually. This creates a more connected process between company spending and expense reporting.

### Reimbursement Management

When employees pay business expenses personally, the system can track approved amounts and support reimbursement workflows.

Businesses can define which expenses qualify for reimbursement and connect approved reports with payment or accounting processes.

### Travel and Mileage Management

Some platforms include travel-management capabilities or integrate with travel-booking systems. Employees can manage flights, hotels, meals, transportation, and other travel expenses within connected workflows.

Mileage and per-diem functionality can also be useful for businesses whose employees regularly travel for work. Microsoft Business Central, for example, supports mileage and per-diem details within expense management.

### Reporting and Analytics

Expense-management systems can provide reports covering employee spending, expense categories, departments, projects, vendors, travel costs, and other financial information.

These reports can help finance teams identify spending patterns and monitor whether expenses are consistent with company policies.

## Business Uses of Expense Management Software

Expense management software can support businesses across many industries.

Travel-heavy organizations can use it to manage airfare, hotels, meals, transportation, and mileage.

Professional services firms can track employee expenses associated with client projects and engagements.

Sales organizations can manage expenses related to customer meetings, events, travel, and business development.

Remote and distributed businesses can provide employees with digital tools for submitting expenses regardless of location.

Finance departments can use centralized expense data for reconciliation, reporting, budgeting, and financial controls.

The appropriate workflow depends on the organization's size, spending patterns, employee structure, and internal policies.

## Benefits of Expense Management Software

One potential benefit is reduced manual data entry. Employees can capture receipts digitally, while automated workflows can move reports through review and approval.

Other potential benefits include:

* Faster approvals: Reports can be routed automatically to the appropriate approvers.
* Better policy compliance: Rules can flag expenses that do not meet company requirements.
* Improved visibility: Finance teams can see spending information in a centralized system.
* Simpler reimbursement: Approved employee expenses can move through standardized reimbursement processes.
* Fewer duplicate records: Corporate card and receipt data can be connected.
* Better audit preparation: Digital records can provide documentation of expenses and approvals.
* More consistent reporting: Standardized categories and workflows can improve expense data.

Microsoft and SAP Concur both describe centralized expense data, automated workflows, policy controls, and reporting as key functions of their expense-management platforms.

## Expense Management and Accounting Software

Expense management software often works alongside accounting and ERP systems rather than replacing them.

Once an expense is approved, the relevant information can be transferred to accounting or financial systems for recording and reconciliation. Integration can reduce duplicate data entry and help finance teams maintain consistent financial records.

For example, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Expense Management integrates expense processes with Finance and Human Resources functionality, while SAP Concur provides integrations with ERP, HR, finance, and accounting systems.

Businesses should therefore consider expense software as part of their broader financial technology environment.

## Key Considerations When Choosing Expense Management Software

### Business Requirements

Start by identifying the expenses the organization needs to manage. Consider employee reimbursements, corporate cards, travel, mileage, per diems, project expenses, and other categories.

A small business with straightforward reimbursements may need fewer features than a multinational organization with complex travel and spending policies.

### Integrations

Check whether the platform integrates with existing accounting, ERP, HR, payroll, corporate card, travel, and payment systems.

Strong integrations can reduce manual work and make expense information easier to reconcile.

### Mobile Access

Employees often incur expenses while traveling or away from their desks. Mobile applications can allow them to photograph receipts, submit expenses, check report status, and respond to requests from managers.

### Security

Expense systems contain financial and employee information. Businesses should evaluate authentication, permissions, encryption, audit logs, data protection, and vendor security practices.

### Reporting

Consider what information finance teams and managers need. Reporting may include spending by employee, department, category, project, location, or time period.

### Pricing

Pricing can vary according to employee numbers, expense volume, modules, integrations, and support. Some providers charge per user or per report, while enterprise platforms may use customized pricing.

Businesses should calculate the expected annual cost and consider implementation, migration, training, and integration expenses as part of the total cost.

## Examples of Expense Management Software

The market includes standalone expense platforms and broader financial systems with expense-management capabilities. Examples include SAP Concur, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Expensify, and Ramp.

Some platforms focus primarily on employee expenses and reimbursements, while others combine expense management with corporate cards, travel, accounts payable, budgeting, and broader spend-management functionality.

Businesses should compare platforms according to their transaction volume, policy complexity, accounting environment, workforce structure, and reporting requirements.

## Final Thoughts

Expense management software helps businesses organize employee spending, automate expense reporting, enforce policies, process reimbursements, and connect expense information with financial systems. Digital receipt capture, approval workflows, corporate card integrations, and reporting can reduce administrative work while improving visibility into company spending.

Before selecting a platform, businesses should map their current expense process and identify the areas that create the most manual work. Evaluating integrations, policy controls, mobile capabilities, security, reporting, scalability, and total cost can help ensure that the software fits both current operations and future growth.
