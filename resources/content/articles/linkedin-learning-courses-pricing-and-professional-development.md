## September 25, 2026 • Emily Carter

LinkedIn Learning is an online professional education platform that provides courses covering business, technology, creative skills, leadership, data, artificial intelligence, and other workplace subjects. The platform combines expert-led instruction with personalized recommendations, learning paths, skills development, and professional credentials.

The service is connected to LinkedIn's broader professional ecosystem, allowing learning activity to support career development and skills discovery. Individual learners can access courses through eligible LinkedIn Premium subscriptions, while companies can purchase LinkedIn Learning for Teams or Enterprise and provide access across their workforce.

For organizations, LinkedIn Learning is positioned as more than a video-course library. Business features include administrative dashboards, learning paths, skills insights, integrations, AI-powered coaching, and tools for connecting employee development with organizational talent strategies.

## What Is LinkedIn Learning?

LinkedIn Learning is LinkedIn's professional learning platform. Its library currently contains more than 26,000 courses, with content from thousands of industry experts and thought leaders. Courses are available across areas such as business, technology, and creative skills, with subtitles available in more than 20 languages.

Learning formats extend beyond conventional video lessons. The platform supports audio and text-based learning, short-form Nano Tips, quizzes, exercise files, coding environments, and project-based learning.

LinkedIn also updates its catalog regularly, making the service suitable for subjects where professional practices and technologies change frequently.

## LinkedIn Learning Course Categories

## Business and Professional Skills

Business courses cover subjects such as management, leadership, communication, project management, sales, marketing, negotiation, productivity, and decision-making.

These courses can support both individual career development and structured corporate training programs.

## Technology and Data

The technology catalog includes programming, databases, cloud computing, cybersecurity, data analysis, machine learning, software development, and other technical subjects.

LinkedIn Learning also has dedicated content around AI literacy and generative AI, covering both general workplace applications and technical topics.

## Creative Skills

Creative courses cover areas such as graphic design, photography, video production, animation, illustration, presentation design, and other creative workflows.

This makes the platform relevant to employees who need to develop both technical and creative capabilities.

## Learning Paths and Role Guides

LinkedIn Learning uses structured learning experiences to help employees progress beyond individual courses.

The platform currently offers more than 1,300 curated learning pathways and Role Guides covering more than 35 roles. These resources organize related courses around particular skills, responsibilities, or career objectives.

Organizations can also create custom Learning Paths. This allows an administrator to combine courses into a sequence designed around a particular role, department, onboarding program, or strategic skill.

Personalized recommendations can further direct learners toward courses based on their interests, roles, learning activity, and skills.

## Hands-On Practice and Credentials

LinkedIn Learning increasingly combines video instruction with practical learning.

The platform currently offers more than 300,000 quiz questions, approximately 10,000 exercise files, and virtual coding environments using GitHub Codespaces. Some courses also provide project-based exercises and other hands-on activities.

Professional development can also include credentials. LinkedIn Learning provides Professional Certificates from selected providers and preparation for more than 120 external credentials through thousands of relevant courses.

Certificates can provide evidence that a learner completed a course, although organizations and professionals should distinguish platform completion certificates from independently issued professional certifications.

## AI-Powered Learning Features

Artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated into LinkedIn Learning.

Current business offerings include AI-powered coaching, immersive role-play experiences, AI-powered search, course companions, and personalized learning plans. These tools are designed to make training more interactive and align learning with specific skills or career objectives.

AI-powered coaching can provide practice for professional situations where interactive exercises may be more useful than passive video instruction. For example, learners can use role-play experiences to practice communication or other workplace scenarios.

LinkedIn also provides AI-focused learning collections designed to help employees understand and apply artificial intelligence in different professional roles.

## LinkedIn Learning for Teams

LinkedIn Learning for Teams is designed for smaller organizations with up to 20 learning accounts.

The current public Teams page lists pricing of $379.88 per seat per year. The offering includes access to the learning library, personalized recommendations, custom Learning Paths, learning-progress insights, course assignments, and collaboration features.

Team administrators can monitor learning activity and identify popular courses or areas of employee engagement. This provides a basic framework for organizations that want centralized professional development without deploying a larger enterprise learning environment.

## LinkedIn Learning Enterprise

Organizations with larger workforces can use LinkedIn Learning Enterprise. LinkedIn's current product information describes Enterprise capabilities including curated learning content, hands-on practice, professional credentials, custom content uploads, Learning Paths, and LMS/LXP integrations.

Enterprise organizations can also connect learning with broader talent-management initiatives. LinkedIn Learning Career Hub adds features such as skills insights, career exploration, personalized development plans, internal job opportunities, and HRIS integrations.

This approach allows companies to connect employee learning with internal mobility and longer-term workforce planning.

## LinkedIn Learning Pricing

Pricing depends on whether access is purchased individually, for a small team, or for an enterprise organization.

For individuals, LinkedIn does not sell individual courses separately. Access is provided through eligible LinkedIn Premium subscriptions, which can include LinkedIn Learning alongside other LinkedIn features.

For small teams, LinkedIn currently lists $379.88 per seat annually for LinkedIn Learning for Teams, with support for up to 20 licenses.

Enterprise pricing is handled through LinkedIn's sales organization rather than a universal public per-user price. Costs can therefore depend on organizational requirements, number of users, and selected capabilities.

Because subscription structures and promotional terms can change, businesses should verify current pricing directly with LinkedIn before purchasing.

## Analytics and Skills Insights

Learning analytics help organizations understand how employees use the platform.

Team dashboards can show learning activity, engagement, and popular courses. Enterprise capabilities extend into skills intelligence and talent architecture, helping organizations identify relevant skills and connect development plans with workforce needs.

LinkedIn also uses information from its broader professional network to provide insights into emerging skills and career development.

These capabilities can be particularly relevant for companies building structured upskilling programs around areas such as AI, data, cybersecurity, and leadership.

## Business Uses for LinkedIn Learning

Organizations can apply LinkedIn Learning to several professional development objectives.

Employee onboarding can introduce new hires to workplace skills and role-specific knowledge.

Technical upskilling can support development in programming, data, cloud computing, cybersecurity, and AI.

Leadership development can provide managers with structured learning in communication, management, and organizational skills.

Career development can connect learning plans with potential future roles and internal opportunities.

AI readiness can help employees build foundational AI literacy or develop more specialized technical capabilities.

## How Businesses Evaluate LinkedIn Learning

Companies evaluating LinkedIn Learning should consider the relevance of its course catalog, learning formats, administrative requirements, reporting capabilities, and integration options.

Smaller teams may focus primarily on course access, recommendations, Learning Paths, and basic analytics. Larger organizations may place greater emphasis on LMS/LXP integrations, custom content, skills intelligence, HRIS connectivity, and career-development capabilities.

The quality of a learning program also depends on how the organization incorporates courses into broader development plans. Simply providing access to a large catalog does not necessarily create a structured training strategy.

## Final Thoughts

LinkedIn Learning combines professional courses with personalized learning, practical exercises, credentials, analytics, and career-development tools. Its catalog spans business, technology, creative disciplines, leadership, data, and artificial intelligence.

Individual access is connected to LinkedIn Premium, while Teams provides a self-service option for smaller organizations and Enterprise supports larger workforce learning programs.

For businesses, the platform can serve as part of a broader professional development strategy, particularly when learning content is combined with structured Learning Paths, skills analysis, employee goals, and internal career opportunities.
