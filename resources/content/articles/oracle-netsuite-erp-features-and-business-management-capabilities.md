Oracle NetSuite is a cloud-based enterprise resource planning platform designed to "connect financial management, inventory, order management, customer relationship management, ecommerce, and other business processes within one system." Its ERP capabilities are intended to give organizations a centralized view of financial and operational activity.

NetSuite supports businesses that need more than standalone accounting or inventory software. Its broader platform can connect transactions across departments, allowing financial information to be linked with purchasing, sales, inventory, projects, employees, and customer activity.

Because NetSuite is modular, businesses can use different capabilities according to their operational requirements. Understanding its ERP features helps organizations evaluate how the platform could fit their financial and business management processes.

## What Is Oracle NetSuite?

Oracle NetSuite is a cloud business management suite that includes ERP and financial management alongside CRM and ecommerce capabilities. The platform is designed to provide a unified environment for managing core business operations.

NetSuite ERP covers areas including accounting, inventory, order management, purchasing, project management, and employee management. Organizations can also extend the platform with additional applications and SuiteApps.

The centralized structure can reduce the need to maintain separate systems for individual business functions and can make information available across departments.

## NetSuite Financial Management

Financial management is a central part of NetSuite ERP. The platform includes accounting functions for managing financial transactions and producing business reports.

Key capabilities include:
* General ledger and chart of accounts management
* Accounts payable and accounts receivable
* Banking and cash management
* Financial statements and reporting
* Revenue recognition
* Tax management
* Fixed asset management
* Multi-book accounting
* Multi-currency support

By connecting financial transactions with operational activity, NetSuite can give finance teams a broader view of business performance.

Financial information can also be used by management for budgeting, forecasting, cash-flow analysis, and other planning activities.

## Inventory Management

NetSuite provides inventory management capabilities for businesses that purchase, store, manufacture, and sell physical products.

The platform can track inventory quantities, costs, locations, and asset values. Inventory transactions can update records as products are purchased, received, sold, transferred, or fulfilled.

Businesses with more complex inventory requirements can use capabilities such as:
* Multi-location inventory
* Serial and lot tracking
* Bin management
* Inventory status
* Demand planning
* Supply allocation
* Available-to-promise
* Advanced inventory management

These features can connect inventory information with purchasing and sales processes, helping organizations maintain more consistent information about stock availability.

## Order Management and Procurement

NetSuite supports the order-to-cash process from customer orders through fulfillment and billing. This connects sales transactions with inventory and financial records.

On the procurement side, businesses can manage purchase orders, vendors, receiving, bills, and payments. Approval workflows can also be used to structure purchasing processes.

Connecting procurement and order management to inventory and accounting can reduce manual data entry and provide greater visibility into transactions across the business.

## NetSuite CRM

Although NetSuite is primarily known as an ERP platform, its suite also includes customer relationship management functionality.

NetSuite CRM can store customer and prospect information and connect sales activity with financial and operational records. Sales teams can manage leads, opportunities, customers, and interactions while other departments can access relevant customer information.

This connection can be particularly useful when businesses want sales activity and financial transactions to operate from the same underlying business system.

## Project Management and Professional Services

NetSuite includes project management capabilities for organizations that deliver services or manage projects.

Businesses can track project tasks, resources, time, expenses, budgets, and financial performance. Project information can be connected with accounting and billing processes.

For professional services organizations, this can provide visibility into project profitability, resource utilization, and customer billing without requiring a completely separate financial system.

## Manufacturing and Supply Chain

NetSuite can also support manufacturing and supply chain processes. Depending on the configuration and additional capabilities enabled, organizations can manage bills of materials, work orders, production processes, inventory, and supply planning.

Manufacturing-related functionality can connect production activity with inventory and financial information. Businesses can therefore track materials and finished goods while maintaining corresponding operational and accounting records.

Advanced supply chain features can support demand planning, material requirements planning, distribution resource planning, and other processes.

## NetSuite OneWorld

For organizations operating across multiple countries or legal entities, "NetSuite OneWorld provides capabilities for managing multinational and multicompany operations."

OneWorld supports multiple subsidiaries, currencies, tax requirements, and accounting standards. It can also consolidate financial information across entities while maintaining local-level information.

This structure is relevant for companies that need both local financial management and consolidated reporting across regions.

## Business Intelligence and Reporting

NetSuite provides reporting and analytics capabilities that allow organizations to examine financial and operational information.

Users can work with dashboards, reports, saved searches, and other analytical tools to monitor business activity. Different employees can receive information relevant to their roles, such as financial performance for executives or inventory information for operations teams.

Because the underlying data is connected across business processes, reporting can combine information from areas such as sales, inventory, purchasing, and finance.

## Automation and Customization

NetSuite includes workflow and automation capabilities that can reduce repetitive manual processes. Businesses can automate approvals, notifications, transaction processing, and other workflows according to their requirements.

The platform can also be customized through configuration, SuiteScript, SuiteFlow, SuiteTalk APIs, and other development tools.

This flexibility allows organizations to adapt NetSuite to specific processes, although more extensive customization can increase implementation and maintenance requirements.

## NetSuite Ecommerce and Customer Operations

NetSuite can connect ecommerce activity with inventory, order management, customer information, and financial processes.

For businesses selling through online channels, this integration can provide a common view of orders and customer activity. Ecommerce transactions can feed into fulfillment and financial workflows rather than being managed entirely in separate systems.

NetSuite can therefore serve as a broader business platform for organizations combining ecommerce with physical inventory and financial operations.

## NetSuite Pricing and Implementation

NetSuite does not generally operate with a simple public price list covering every customer configuration. Costs depend on the selected edition, modules, number of users, implementation requirements, and other factors.

Businesses should therefore evaluate more than the base software subscription when estimating total cost. Implementation, customization, integrations, training, support, and additional modules can all affect the overall investment.

A structured implementation process is particularly important for ERP systems because financial, operational, and customer data often need to be migrated and standardized.

## How Businesses Evaluate NetSuite

Organizations evaluating NetSuite typically consider the complexity of their financial operations, number of entities, inventory requirements, international operations, ecommerce needs, project management requirements, and integration environment.

Companies should also determine which capabilities are essential and which require additional modules or customization.

The quality of implementation can be as important as the software itself. Businesses should define processes, data structures, user permissions, integrations, and reporting requirements before deployment.

## Final Thoughts

Oracle NetSuite combines ERP, financial management, inventory, order management, CRM, ecommerce, project management, and other business capabilities within a cloud platform.

Its centralized approach can help organizations connect financial and operational processes while providing broader visibility across departments. At the same time, the breadth of the platform means businesses should carefully evaluate modules, customization, implementation requirements, and total costs.

For organizations looking to consolidate multiple business processes into a connected ERP environment, NetSuite provides a broad set of tools that can be configured around different operational models.
