Microsoft 365 is a cloud-based productivity platform that combines familiar Office applications with business email, cloud storage, collaboration, communication, security, and administrative tools. Instead of purchasing individual desktop applications separately, organizations can use subscription plans that combine multiple services under centralized account management.

For businesses, Microsoft 365 can support everyday activities such as writing documents, analyzing spreadsheets, creating presentations, managing email, organizing files, communicating with teams, and coordinating projects. The platform also integrates with Microsoft's broader security, identity, automation, and artificial intelligence services.

Microsoft offers several business subscriptions, with differences in desktop applications, collaboration capabilities, security controls, and AI features. Understanding those differences can help organizations match their subscription to their workforce, technical requirements, and budget.

## What Is Microsoft 365?

Microsoft 365 is a subscription platform built around applications and services including Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, OneDrive, SharePoint, Microsoft Teams, and Exchange Online. Depending on the plan, users can access applications through desktop, web, and mobile versions.

Business plans are generally designed for organizations with up to 300 users, while Microsoft's enterprise plans address larger or more complex environments. Microsoft also provides administration and identity-management capabilities that allow organizations to manage users, devices, applications, and access policies centrally.

## Microsoft 365 Business Plans

Microsoft currently offers several business-oriented options, including Business Basic, Business Standard, and Business Premium, as well as Microsoft 365 Apps for business.

## Microsoft 365 Business Basic

Business Basic is designed around web and mobile productivity. It includes Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, OneDrive, Exchange, SharePoint, Teams, and additional services such as Planner, Forms, and Bookings.

The US annual price is currently $7 per user per month with Teams. A no-Teams version is also available at a lower price. Users receive 1 TB of OneDrive storage per user and business email with a custom domain.

## Microsoft 365 Business Standard

Business Standard adds the desktop versions of Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and other applications to the services available with Basic. It also includes tools such as Microsoft Loop and Clipchamp.

The US annual price currently starts at $14 per user per month for the standard Teams-inclusive plan. This makes the subscription relevant for organizations that need full desktop Office applications alongside cloud collaboration.

## Microsoft 365 Business Premium

Business Premium combines the productivity applications in Standard with additional security, device management, identity, and threat-protection capabilities.

The current US price is $22 per user per month when paid annually for the Teams-inclusive plan. Microsoft positions Premium around organizations that require stronger security and administration alongside productivity software.

## Microsoft 365 Apps for business

Microsoft 365 Apps for business focuses primarily on the Office applications and cloud storage rather than the complete collection of business communication and management services. The current US annual price is $10 per user per month.

It provides desktop versions of applications such as Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook, together with 1 TB of OneDrive storage per user.

## Word, Excel, and PowerPoint

The core Microsoft 365 applications remain important for many business workflows.

Word supports document creation, editing, formatting, collaboration, and professional business documentation.

Excel provides spreadsheets, formulas, charts, data analysis, financial modeling, and reporting capabilities. Businesses can use it for budgeting, forecasting, operational tracking, and analysis.

PowerPoint is designed for presentations, training materials, sales decks, internal meetings, and customer-facing communications.

With Microsoft 365 subscriptions that include desktop applications, users can work across desktop, web, and mobile environments while keeping files synchronized through Microsoft's cloud services.

## Outlook and Exchange Online

Microsoft 365 also provides business email through Exchange Online, with Outlook acting as the primary interface for email, calendars, contacts, and tasks.

Business plans support custom domain addresses, allowing organizations to use addresses such as name@company.com rather than consumer email domains. Exchange Online also provides centralized administration and business-oriented email protection.

Outlook can connect email, meetings, calendars, files, and contacts into a single workflow, making it useful for employees who manage substantial amounts of communication and scheduling.

## OneDrive and SharePoint

OneDrive provides individual cloud storage, with business plans generally including 1 TB per user. Employees can store, synchronize, edit, and share files across supported devices.

SharePoint focuses more on organizational and team-level content. Companies can create team sites, document libraries, intranet-style resources, and structured repositories for shared information.

Together, OneDrive and SharePoint provide a cloud-based foundation for storing and collaborating on Microsoft 365 documents.

## Microsoft Teams

Microsoft Teams combines chat, meetings, calling, file collaboration, and team workspaces.

Employees can create channels around departments, projects, or other business activities. Teams also connects with Microsoft 365 applications, allowing employees to collaborate on documents and organize meetings without constantly switching between unrelated services.

Depending on the subscription, businesses can also access webinar and meeting capabilities designed for larger internal or external events.

## Productivity and Workflow Tools

Microsoft 365 includes several additional applications that can support operational workflows.

Microsoft Planner helps teams organize tasks and projects, while Microsoft To Do focuses on individual task management. Microsoft Lists can be used to structure business information in customizable lists.

Microsoft Forms supports surveys, questionnaires, and data collection. Microsoft Bookings can help organizations manage appointments and scheduling.

Microsoft Loop provides collaborative workspaces where teams can organize ideas, information, and project content. Clipchamp provides video editing capabilities for business content.

## Microsoft 365 and AI

Artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated into Microsoft's productivity ecosystem through Microsoft Copilot.

Copilot can work within applications such as Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and Teams, depending on the subscription and licensing configuration. Microsoft describes its business AI capabilities as being grounded in organizational work context and supported by Work IQ.

Microsoft 365 Copilot Business is also available as an additional subscription for eligible Microsoft 365 customers. Microsoft's current promotional pricing lists Copilot Business at $18 per user per month paid annually, subject to the applicable offer terms.

## Security and Administration

Microsoft 365 Business Premium adds capabilities intended for organizations with more demanding security requirements. These include identity and access management, device protection, advanced threat protection, and information protection.

Administrators can manage users and access centrally, apply security policies, and monitor aspects of an organization's Microsoft 365 environment. Microsoft also provides tools such as Secure Score and Adoption Score to provide additional visibility into security posture and service adoption.

## Microsoft 365 Pricing Considerations

Microsoft 365 pricing depends on the selected plan, billing frequency, whether Teams is included, and whether additional services such as Copilot are purchased.

Current US annual business pricing provides a useful starting point:
* Business Basic: $7/user/month
* Business Standard: $14/user/month
* Business Premium: $22/user/month
* Microsoft 365 Apps for business: $10/user/month
* Microsoft 365 Copilot Business: currently promoted from $21 to $18/user/month annually

Microsoft periodically changes pricing and promotional offers, so organizations should verify current pricing before purchasing.

## How Businesses Evaluate Microsoft 365

Organizations typically evaluate Microsoft 365 based on several factors:
* Required desktop and web applications
* Number of employees and supported users
* Email and collaboration requirements
* Cloud storage needs
* Security and device-management requirements
* Existing Microsoft infrastructure
* AI and Copilot requirements
* Administration and compliance needs
* Total subscription and implementation costs

The choice between plans generally depends on which combination of productivity, communication, storage, security, and AI capabilities an organization requires.

## Final Thoughts

Microsoft 365 brings productivity applications, business email, cloud storage, collaboration, communication, workflow tools, security, and AI capabilities into a subscription-based ecosystem. Business Basic focuses on essential cloud productivity, Standard adds desktop Office applications, and Premium extends the platform with stronger security and management capabilities.

For organizations already using Microsoft technologies, the platform can provide a common environment for documents, communication, collaboration, and business information. Evaluating the available plans against actual user requirements is important because not every employee necessarily needs the same combination of applications and security capabilities.
